# CHAPTER 1: HOW CREDIT SCORES ACTUALLY WORK

## The Five Factors — And What They Really Mean

Your FICO score (used by 90% of lenders) is calculated from five factors:

| Factor | Weight | What It Means |
|--------|--------|---------------|
| Payment History | 35% | Have you paid on time? |
| Credit Utilization | 30% | How much of your available credit are you using? |
| Length of Credit History | 15% | How old are your accounts? |
| Credit Mix | 10% | Do you have different types of credit? |
| New Credit | 10% | Have you recently applied for credit? |

### The Real Strategy

**Payment History (35%):** This is non-negotiable. One 30-day late payment can drop your score 60–110 points. Pay everything on time, even if it's the minimum payment. Set autopay today.

**Credit Utilization (30%):** This is the fastest lever you control. Keep utilization under 10% for maximum score impact. Under 30% is acceptable. Above 30% tanks your score. Pay down balances before your statement closing date — not the due date — because the closing date balance is what gets reported to bureaus.

**Length of History (15%):** Don't close old accounts. A 10-year-old credit card you don't use is worth more than you think — it keeps your average age high. If you must close accounts, close the newest ones.

**Credit Mix (10%):** Lenders want to see you can manage different types of credit. Revolving (credit cards) + installment (loans) + open (charge cards) = ideal mix. A credit builder loan adds installment credit to a profile that only has cards.

**New Credit (10%):** Each hard inquiry drops your score 3–10 points and stays for 2 years. Apply strategically, not impulsively. Rate-shop for mortgages/auto loans within a 14–45 day window — multiple inquiries for the same type count as one.

# CHAPTER 3: THE DISPUTE SYSTEM

## Phase 1: Audit Your Reports

Pull all three credit reports at **AnnualCreditReport.com** — the only federally mandated free source.

For each negative item, record:
- Creditor/Collector name
- Account number
- Date of first delinquency (critical for statute of limitations)
- Amount reported
- Status (collection, charged-off, late, etc.)
- Which bureaus report it

## Phase 2: Prioritize Your Disputes

Attack in this order for maximum score impact:

1. **Collections** — each one suppresses your score heavily
2. **Late payments** — especially recent ones (last 2 years)
3. **Charge-offs** — high-balance charge-offs tank scores
4. **Inquiries** — unauthorized ones are worth removing
5. **Outdated items** — anything past the 7-year mark

## Phase 3: Execute the Dispute Strategy

### Route A — Bureau Disputes (FCRA)
Send to Equifax, Experian, TransUnion directly. Use certified mail.
Best for: Items that are inaccurate, unverifiable, or expired.

### Route B — Creditor/Collector Disputes (FDCPA)
Send to the original creditor or current collector.
Best for: Requesting validation, pay-for-delete negotiation.

### Route C — CFPB Complaint
File at consumerfinance.gov/complaint.
Best for: When bureaus "verify" without actually investigating. A CFPB complaint creates pressure.

### Route D — Legal Action
When all else fails, or when violations are clear.
Best for: FDCPA violations, clear FCRA willful noncompliance.

## Phase 4: The 30-Day Cycle

```
Day 1: Send disputes (certified mail)
Day 5-7: Bureau receives letter (allow mail time)
Day 35: Investigation period ends (30 days from receipt)
Day 37: If no response → follow up with stronger letter
Day 45: Final deadline for disputes with additional info submitted
Day 46: If not resolved → escalate (CFPB complaint or legal)
```

# CHAPTER 6: THE BUSINESS CREDIT BLUEPRINT

## Why Business Credit Is the Real Game

Personal credit is limited. Business credit is different:
- Operates on its own scoring system (D&B Paydex, Experian Biz, Equifax Biz)
- **Not tied to your personal SSN** once established
- Can reach $50,000–$500,000+ in credit lines
- Does NOT appear on your personal credit report
- Builds while you're building personal credit simultaneously

## Phase 1: Business Foundation (Month 1–2)

**Incorporation:**
- Form an LLC (or S-Corp) in your state
- LegalZoom, ZenBusiness, or your state's online filing portal
- Cost: $50–$500 depending on state

**Get Your EIN:**
- Apply at IRS.gov/EIN — free, takes 5 minutes
- This is your business's "Social Security Number"

**Business Address:**
- Use a real address (not a P.O. Box for primary)
- Virtual office services work (Regus, iPostal1) — $10–$50/mo

**Business Phone:**
- Dedicated line (Google Voice free, or business phone plan)
- Must match what's in your business profile

**Business Bank Account:**
- Open at a local credit union or online bank (Relay, Mercury)
- Keep personal and business finances 100% separate

**DUNS Number:**
- Register at Dun & Bradstreet (dnb.com) — free
- This activates your D&B Paydex score
- D&B Paydex 80 = good standing

## Phase 2: Net-30 Vendors (Month 2–3)
