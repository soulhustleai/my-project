## LAW 1 — THE FAIR CREDIT REPORTING ACT (FCRA)
**15 U.S.C. § 1681**

### What It Is
The FCRA is your foundational weapon. It governs everything on your credit report — who can see it, how long items stay, and most importantly, **your right to dispute anything inaccurate.**

### What It Gives You
- The right to a **free credit report** from all three bureaus every 12 months
- The right to **dispute any inaccurate, incomplete, or unverifiable information**
- Bureaus have **30 days** to investigate your dispute (sometimes 45)
- If they can't verify it — **they must remove it**
- You can sue for **$100–$1,000 per violation** (statutory damages) — no proof of harm needed

### The Power Move
Under FCRA § 1681e(b), credit bureaus must follow "reasonable procedures" to ensure accuracy. They often don't. This means **most negative items are disputable** — not because the debt isn't real, but because the *reporting* isn't accurate.

**Key Section:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681i — your dispute rights
**Who to contact:** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion directly

## LAW 2 — THE FAIR DEBT COLLECTION PRACTICES ACT (FDCPA)
**15 U.S.C. § 1692**

### What It Is
Debt collectors are not above the law. The FDCPA puts hard limits on what they can do — and **when they cross the line, you get paid.**

### What It Gives You
- They **cannot** call before 8am or after 9pm
- They **cannot** harass, threaten, or use abusive language
- They **cannot** contact your employer (in most cases)
- They **must** send a written validation notice within 5 days of first contact
- You can **demand they stop contacting you** in writing — they legally must comply
- You can sue for **up to $1,000 per lawsuit** plus actual damages and attorney fees

### The Power Move
Send a **Debt Validation Letter** (certified mail, return receipt) within 30 days of their first contact. They must prove the debt is yours, the amount is correct, and they have the right to collect. If they can't — **they must stop collection activity.**

**Key Section:** 15 U.S.C. § 1692g — debt validation rights
**Pro tip:** Keep records of every call. Screenshot everything. One violation = potential lawsuit.

## LAW 3 — THE FAIR AND ACCURATE CREDIT TRANSACTIONS ACT (FACTA)
**Public Law 108-159**

### What It Is
FACTA is an amendment to the FCRA, and it added serious new weapons to your arsenal — including the right to fraud alerts and one of the most powerful deletion tools available.

### What It Gives You
- The right to place a **free fraud alert** on your credit file (lenders must verify your identity before issuing credit)
- **Extended fraud alerts** (7 years) for identity theft victims — comes with 2 free credit reports per year
- The **right to block** fraudulent information from appearing on your report
- Creditors must **truncate** card numbers on receipts (protect your data)
- Your employer cannot pull your full credit report without your consent

### The Power Move
If you've ever been a victim of identity theft — or even suspect it — you can file an identity theft report with the FTC and use FACTA to **block entire accounts** from your credit report. This is faster than disputing through the standard FCRA process.

**Key Section:** 15 U.S.C. § 1681c-2 — blocking fraudulent information
**Resource:** IdentityTheft.gov (FTC official reporting tool)

## LAW 4 — THE EQUAL CREDIT OPPORTUNITY ACT (ECOA)
**15 U.S.C. § 1691**

### What It Is
The ECOA makes it illegal for creditors to discriminate against you based on race, color, religion, national origin, sex, marital status, age, or because you receive public assistance.

### What It Gives You
- **Equal access to credit** regardless of demographics
- The right to know **why you were denied** credit (in writing, within 60 days)
- The right to know **why your credit limit was decreased** or account closed
- Protection against **discriminatory lending practices**
- The right to sue for actual damages, punitive damages up to $10,000, and attorney fees

### The Power Move
When you're denied credit, **always request the adverse action notice in writing.** It must state the specific reasons. If those reasons don't match your actual credit profile — or if you suspect discrimination — you have grounds for a complaint with the CFPB or a lawsuit.

**Key Section:** 15 U.S.C. § 1691(d) — right to notification
**Where to complain:** consumerfinance.gov/complaint

## LAW 5 — THE FAIR CREDIT BILLING ACT (FCBA)
**15 U.S.C. § 1666**

### What It Is
The FCBA protects you from billing errors on credit card accounts — and gives you a formal process to dispute charges that is *separate* from and more powerful than a standard chargeback.

### What It Gives You
- The right to dispute **billing errors** (unauthorized charges, wrong amounts, goods not delivered)
- Creditor has **30 days** to acknowledge your dispute
- Creditor has **90 days** to resolve it
- While under dispute, they **cannot** report it as delinquent to credit bureaus
- They **cannot** close your account or restrict it while a good-faith dispute is pending
- You can sue for **twice the amount of the finance charge** (min $100, max $1,000)

### The Power Move
Send your dispute **in writing** (not by phone) to the billing inquiries address — **not** the payment address. Do it within 60 days of the statement with the error. This triggers your full legal protections under FCBA.

**Key Section:** 15 U.S.C. § 1666(a) — billing error dispute process
**Pro tip:** Keep a copy of everything. Certified mail with return receipt creates a paper trail.
