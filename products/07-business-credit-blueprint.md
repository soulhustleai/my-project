## Introduction: Why Business Credit Changes Everything

Most people spend years trying to fix personal credit when the real wealth is on the other side: business credit.

Business credit:
- Is NOT tied to your Social Security Number (once established)
- Does NOT appear on your personal credit report
- Can reach $50,000–$500,000+ in available credit lines
- Has its own scoring system separate from FICO
- Can be built simultaneously with personal credit repair

The same banks that said no to you personally will say yes to your business — because businesses are evaluated differently. They look at your business's payment history, your Paydex score, your time in business, and your revenue.

This blueprint gives you the complete system from LLC formation to funded business.

# PART 1: BUILD THE FOUNDATION

## Step 1: Form Your Business Entity

**Recommended: LLC (Limited Liability Company)**

Why an LLC:
- Limits personal liability
- Pass-through taxation (profit taxed as personal income, avoiding double taxation)
- Flexible management structure
- Easiest to form and maintain

**How to Form:**
1. Choose your state (Delaware, Wyoming, and Nevada have business-friendly laws — or just use your home state)
2. Choose a name (check availability at your state's Secretary of State website)
3. File Articles of Organization ($50–$500 depending on state)
4. Get an Operating Agreement (free templates available, or use LegalZoom)
5. Open a business bank account in the LLC's name

**Formation services:** ZenBusiness ($49 + state fee), LegalZoom, or file directly with your state.

## Step 2: Get Your EIN

**EIN = Employer Identification Number**

This is your business's tax ID number — the equivalent of a Social Security Number for your business. Required for:
- Business bank accounts
- Business credit cards
- Hiring employees
- Tax filing
- Business credit applications

**How to Get It:**
- Go to IRS.gov/EIN
- Complete the online application
- Takes 5 minutes
- Completely free
- Received instantly online (print the confirmation page)

## Step 3: Establish Business Identity

For business credit to build, your business must have a consistent, credible identity across all platforms. This is called your **business credibility profile**.

### Business Address
- **Do NOT use a P.O. Box** as your primary address (raises flags with lenders)
- Use your real business address, or a virtual office
- Virtual office options: iPostal1 ($10-15/mo), Regus ($50-100/mo), Alliance Virtual Offices
- Must match across: IRS, Secretary of State, DUNS, all applications

### Business Phone Number
- Dedicated business line (not your personal cell listed as business)
- Google Voice: Free (but may be flagged by some lenders)
- Professional option: RingCentral, Grasshopper ($26/mo)
- List it in 411/business directories (use ListYourself.net or ExpressUpdateUSA)

### Business Email
- Use your domain email (name@yourbusiness.com), not Gmail
- Google Workspace: $6/month

### Business Website
- Basic professional website required for many business credit applications
- Squarespace, Wix, or Webflow — even a 1-page site counts
- Must include: business name, address, phone, description of services

### NAICS Code
- Find the correct NAICS code for your business type at census.gov/naics
- Use consistently on all applications

## Step 4: Get Your DUNS Number

**DUNS = D&B Data Universal Numbering System**

Dun & Bradstreet is the primary business credit bureau. Your DUNS number activates your D&B credit file and Paydex score.

**How to Get It:**
1. Go to dnb.com
2. Search for your business (it may already exist)
3. If not found, register for a new DUNS number
4. Free registration available
5. Allow 30 days for full profile activation

**D&B Paydex Score:**
- Range: 1–100 (80+ = good, 100 = perfect)
- Based solely on payment history
- Paying NET-30 invoices = Paydex 80
- Paying NET-10 (early) = Paydex 90+
- One late payment = significant drop

# PART 3: FUNDING SOURCES

## Small Business Administration (SBA) Loans

The SBA doesn't lend money directly — it guarantees loans made by approved lenders. This reduces risk for lenders and results in lower rates and better terms for borrowers.

### SBA 7(a) Loan
**Best for:** Working capital, equipment, real estate, business acquisition
- Loan amount: Up to $5 million
- Terms: 10 years (working capital), 25 years (real estate)
- Rates: Prime + 2.25%–4.75%
- Requirements: 2+ years in business, 680+ personal credit, demonstrated revenue
- Use case: Scaling an established business

### SBA Microloan Program
**Best for:** Startups, very small businesses needing under $50K
- Loan amount: Up to $50,000 (average: $13,000)
- Terms: Up to 6 years
- Rates: 8%–13%
- Requirements: No strict minimums, credit review varies by lender
- Use case: First business loan, startup capital

### SBA Express Loan
**Best for:** Faster approval needed
- Loan amount: Up to $500,000
- Turnaround: 36 hours (vs. weeks for standard)
- Same general requirements as 7(a)

**How to Apply:**
1. Go to SBA.gov → Find Lenders (SBSS tool)
2. Identify SBA-preferred lenders in your area
3. Prepare: 2 years tax returns (personal + business), P&L, balance sheet, business plan
4. Apply at the lender (bank, credit union, or online)

## Shelf Corporations (Advanced Strategy)

A shelf corporation is an aged business entity that has been "sitting on the shelf" — legally registered but not actively operated. Purchasing one gives you:
- Instant business age (2, 5, 10+ year history)
- May come with existing credit profile (higher tier)
- Bank and lender credibility (older = lower risk)

**Cost:** $2,000–$10,000+ depending on age and credit profile
**Sources:** CorpNet, Wholesale Shelves, various brokers

**CRITICAL WARNINGS:**
- Verify the corporation has no hidden liabilities before purchasing
- Work with a business attorney on the transfer
- Don't misrepresent the corporation's history to lenders
- Some lenders are wise to shelf corps — do your research per lender
