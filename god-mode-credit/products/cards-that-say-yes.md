### 2. Self (formerly Self Lender)
**What it is:** A credit builder savings account. You make monthly payments (building credit), and at the end of the term, you get the money back (minus interest/fees). You're literally saving money AND building credit at the same time.

**How it works:** Choose a plan ($25-$150/month). Make payments for 12-24 months. Each payment reports to all three bureaus. At the end, you receive the total you paid (minus fees) as a lump sum.

**Cost:** $25-$150/month (you get most back at the end)
**Credit check:** Soft pull only (doesn't affect your score)
**Reports to:** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion (all three!)
**Best for:** Building a diverse credit profile (this counts as an installment loan, which is different from a credit card)

**Get the latest signup links at godmodecredit.com/tools**

### 3. Chime Credit Builder Card
**What it is:** A secured credit card with no credit check and no annual fee. You load money onto it, then spend that money — and Chime reports it as credit card activity.

**How it works:** Open a Chime checking account (free). Set up direct deposit. Move money to your Credit Builder card. Spend it. Chime pays it off and reports to all three bureaus.

**Cost:** $0 (free — you spend your own money)
**Credit check:** None
**Reports to:** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
**Requires:** Chime checking account with direct deposit
**Best for:** People who want a "real" credit card experience without the risk

### 4. CreditStrong
**What it is:** Similar to Self — a credit builder account that combines a small loan with a savings account. Your payments build credit AND build savings.

**How it works:** Choose a plan. Make monthly payments. Payments report to all three bureaus. At the end, you've built credit AND saved money.

**Cost:** Plans from $15-$110/month
**Credit check:** None
**Reports to:** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
**Best for:** People who want to build credit AND savings simultaneously

**Get the latest signup links at godmodecredit.com/tools**

### 5. Grow Credit
**What it is:** Build credit by paying subscriptions you already have — Netflix, Spotify, Hulu, etc.

**How it works:** Grow Credit pays your subscription on your behalf, then you pay Grow Credit. They report the payment to the credit bureaus. You're building credit with money you're already spending.

**Cost:** Free plan (1 subscription), paid plans for more
**Credit check:** None
**Reports to:** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
**Best for:** People who already pay for streaming services (so... everyone)

### 6. Extra Card
**What it is:** A debit card that reports to credit bureaus like a credit card. You spend YOUR money, but it counts as credit activity.

**How it works:** Connect your bank account. Spend with the Extra card. Extra reports your spending to credit bureaus as if it were credit card payments.

**Cost:** $8-$25/month
**Credit check:** None
**Reports to:** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
**Best for:** People who want credit building without any debt

These require a security deposit (usually $200-$300) that becomes your credit limit. You're borrowing your OWN money, so the risk to the bank is zero — which is why they approve almost everyone.

### 7. OpenSky Secured Visa
**Why it's special:** NO CREDIT CHECK at all. Not even a soft pull. This card approves literally everyone with a pulse and $200.

**Deposit:** $200-$3,000 (becomes your credit limit)
**Annual fee:** $35
**Credit check:** NONE
**Reports to:** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
**Best for:** People who've been denied everywhere else

### 8. Discover it Secured
**Why it's special:** Earns cash back (2% at gas/restaurants, 1% everything else). And after 7-8 months of on-time payments, Discover AUTOMATICALLY reviews you for graduation to an unsecured card — and gives your deposit back.

**Deposit:** $200-$2,500
**Annual fee:** $0
**Credit check:** Yes (but they're lenient)
**Reports to:** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
**Best for:** People who want a card that graduates to a REAL credit card

### 9. Capital One Platinum Secured
**Why it's special:** Capital One sometimes offers a lower deposit than the credit limit — meaning you might put down $49 and get a $200 limit.

**Deposit:** $49-$200
**Annual fee:** $0
**Credit check:** Yes
**Reports to:** Equifax, Experian, TransUnion
**Best for:** People who don't have $200 for a full deposit
