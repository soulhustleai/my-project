# PART 1: THE ANALYSIS PHASE — AI SEES WHAT YOU MISS

## Step 1: Pull Your Reports (The Human Part — 10 Minutes)

Go to AnnualCreditReport.com. Pull all three. This is the ONLY part where AI can't do the work for you because the bureaus need to verify your identity.

But from here on? AI takes the wheel.

## Step 2: The AI Triple-Scan Method

Don't just read your report. Let three different AI systems analyze it independently, then compare.

### Scan 1: Dovly (Automated Analysis)
Upload your reports to Dovly. Its AI immediately identifies:
- Items eligible for dispute (and ranks them by likelihood of removal)
- Accounts with reporting errors
- Collections that may be time-barred
- Duplicate accounts
- Incorrect personal information

### Scan 2: Dispute AI (Strategic Analysis)
Import the same reports into Dispute AI. It identifies:
- The best dispute REASON for each item (this is its superpower)
- Items the bureau is most likely to delete
- Strategic sequencing (what to dispute first for maximum impact)

### Scan 3: ChatGPT/Claude (Deep Analysis)
This is where you go deeper than any tool can. Use this prompt:

```
I'm going to describe my complete credit situation. I need
you to act as an expert credit analyst and identify EVERY
opportunity for improvement — including things that
automated dispute tools might miss.

PERSONAL INFO:
- Current scores: [Equifax/Experian/TransUnion]
- Number of total accounts: [X]
- Number of negative items: [X]
- Oldest account age: [X years]
- Average account age: [X years]
- Current credit utilization: [X%]
- Hard inquiries (last 2 years): [X]

NEGATIVE ITEMS (list each one):
1. [Type] — [Creditor] — $[Amount] — Opened [Date] —
   Status: [Current status] — Last activity: [Date]
2. [Continue for all items]

POSITIVE ACCOUNTS:
1. [Type] — [Creditor] — Limit $[Amount] — Balance $[X]
   — Opened [Date] — Payment history: [on time or not]
2. [Continue for all accounts]

Please analyze and provide:
1. A prioritized list of ALL items to dispute (ranked by
   impact on score)
2. The specific dispute strategy for EACH item
3. Items that might be improperly reported (dates don't
   add up, balances don't make sense, etc.)
4. Credit utilization optimization strategy
5. New accounts I should open to strengthen my profile
6. Accounts I should NOT close and why
7. An estimated score trajectory if everything goes
   according to plan
8. Anything unusual or suspicious about my report
9. State-specific laws that might help my situation
   (I live in [your state])
10. A 90-day action plan customized to MY situation
```

Here's what happens when you compare all three analyses:


## AI-Generated CFPB Complaints

When a bureau says "verified" without doing real investigation, escalate to the CFPB. Use this prompt:

```
A credit bureau [name] claimed to have "verified" a
disputed item on my report, but I believe the
investigation was inadequate. Here's the situation:

Original dispute: [what you disputed and when]
Bureau response: [what they said]
Method of verification response: [what they said, if you
requested it]

Please draft a CFPB complaint that:
1. Clearly states the timeline of events
2. Cites the specific FCRA sections the bureau violated
3. Explains why the investigation was inadequate
4. Requests specific relief (deletion of item, proper
   investigation)
5. Is professional but firm
6. Is formatted for submission at
   consumerfinance.gov/complaint
```

## The Statement Date Strategy (AI Timing Play)

Here's something most people don't know: your credit utilization is reported to the bureaus on your **statement closing date**, not your payment due date. This means you can strategically time your payments to show low utilization even if you use your cards heavily.

```
AI PROMPT: "My credit cards have the following statement
closing dates:

- Card 1: [date]
- Card 2: [date]
- Card 3: [date]

I want my utilization to show as 1-3% on each card when
reported. Please create a payment schedule telling me
exactly when to pay and how much to leave on each card."
```

## Play #3: The AI Inquiry Removal Strategy

```
"I have [X] hard inquiries on my credit report from the
last 2 years. Here they are:

1. [Company] — [Date] — Did I apply? [Yes/No]
2. [Company] — [Date] — Did I apply? [Yes/No]
3. [Continue]

For each inquiry I did NOT authorize:
1. Draft an inquiry removal letter citing 15 U.S.C. § 1681b
2. Explain my rights regarding unauthorized pulls
3. Should I also file with the CFPB?

For inquiries I DID authorize:
1. Is there any legitimate way to get these removed?
2. Rate shopping — do any of these count as duplicates?
3. When will each one naturally fall off? (2-year rule)"
```

## Play #4: The UCC + AI Power Move

For debt buyers who can't prove they own your debt:

```
"A company called [name] is collecting on a debt they
claim to have purchased from [original creditor]. The
amount is $[X]. I want to use UCC Article 3 to challenge
their standing.

Please:
1. Explain the UCC holder in due course doctrine as it
   applies to my situation
2. Draft a demand letter requiring them to produce:
   - The original negotiable instrument (UCC § 3-301)
   - Complete chain of assignment documentation
   - Bill of sale for this specific account
   - Power of attorney if collecting on behalf of another
3. Explain what happens when they can't produce these
   (spoiler: most can't because the original note was
   destroyed during securitization)
4. Draft a follow-up letter to the credit bureaus if the
   collector can't produce documentation, demanding
   deletion
5. Calculate whether this debt is past the statute of
   limitations in [my state] for [debt type]"
```
