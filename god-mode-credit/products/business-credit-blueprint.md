# CHAPTER 1: THE FOUNDATION — YOUR BUSINESS ENTITY

## Step 1: Form Your LLC (Even If You Don't Have a Business Idea Yet)

An LLC (Limited Liability Company) is the most common and simplest business structure. You don't need a fancy lawyer. You don't need to sell anything yet. You just need the entity to exist.

### Where to Form

| State | Filing Fee | Why People Choose It |
|-------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Your home state** | Varies ($50-$500) | Simplest, no foreign registration needed |
| **Wyoming** | $100 | Best privacy, no state income tax, low fees |
| **Delaware** | $90 | Most business-friendly laws, Court of Chancery |
| **New Mexico** | $50 | Cheapest, no annual report required |

> **Recommendation:** If you live in a state with reasonable fees, just file there. If your state is expensive (California charges $800/year minimum), consider Wyoming or New Mexico.

### How to File (Step by Step)

**Option 1: File yourself (cheapest)**
1. Go to your state's Secretary of State website
2. Search for "form an LLC" or "business filing"
3. Fill out the Articles of Organization:
   - **Company name:** Must be unique in your state. Search availability on the SOS website first. Include "LLC" at the end.
   - **Registered agent:** This can be you (your home address) or a registered agent service ($50-$150/year for privacy)
   - **Member/Manager:** You (as the sole member/manager)
   - **Business address:** Your home is fine to start. Get a virtual address later for privacy.
4. Pay the filing fee
5. Wait for approval (instant to 2 weeks depending on state)

**Option 2: Use a formation service**
- Northwest Registered Agent (~$39 + state fee) — includes registered agent for 1 year
- ZenBusiness (~$0 + state fee) — basic plan is free, you pay state fees
- LegalZoom (~$79 + state fee) — more expensive, not necessary

> **The anecdote:** Don't overthink the business name. Your first LLC doesn't need to be your forever brand. Andre spent 3 months trying to pick the perfect name and missed 3 months of credit building time. Just pick something. You can always form another LLC later or file a DBA (Doing Business As). The entity just needs to EXIST so you can start building credit on it.

### After Formation — The Immediate Checklist

## Step 2: Get Your EIN (Free, Instant, No Excuses)

An EIN (Employer Identification Number) is basically a Social Security Number for your business. You need it for everything — bank accounts, credit applications, vendor accounts, tax filings.

**How to get it:**
1. Go to **irs.gov/businesses/small-businesses-self-employed/apply-for-an-employer-identification-number-ein-online**
2. Click "Apply Online Now"
3. Answer the questions (business type, member info, etc.)
4. Submit
5. **You get your EIN immediately on screen.** Download and save the confirmation letter.

**Cost:** $0. Free. Always has been.

**Time:** 5 minutes.

> **WARNING:** There are websites that charge $79-$299 to "get you an EIN." They are just filling out the same free IRS form and charging you for it. Do NOT pay anyone for this. Go directly to irs.gov.

# CHAPTER 2: THE BUSINESS CREDIT BUREAUS — YOUR NEW SCORECARDS

Your personal credit has Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Your BUSINESS has its own bureaus.

## Dun & Bradstreet (D&B) — The King

**What it is:** The oldest and most respected business credit bureau. Your D&B score is called the **Paydex Score** (0-100). You want 80+.

**How Paydex works:**
- 80 = Pays on time (the minimum "good" score)
- 90 = Pays 20 days early
- 100 = Pays 30 days early
- Below 80 = Pays late (bad)

**How to register:**
1. Go to **dnb.com**
2. Search for your business (it may already have a D-U-N-S number if you registered with your state)
3. If not found, request a free D-U-N-S number
4. Verification takes 1-3 weeks (free) or you can pay $229 for expedited (not necessary)

> **Key insight:** Paydex score ONLY counts if you have trade accounts that report to D&B. Registering alone doesn't give you a score. You need vendor accounts (next chapter).

## Experian Business

**What it is:** Separate from your personal Experian. Business credit score ranges from 1-100. You want 75+.

**How to register:**
- Experian automatically creates your business profile when vendors report to it
- You don't "sign up" — you just build credit and it appears
- Monitor at **smartbusinessreports.com** or through Nav.com

## Equifax Business

**What it is:** Business credit score ranges from 101-992. You want 700+.

**How to register:**
- Similar to Experian — automatically created when data is reported
- Monitor through Nav.com or directly at Equifax

## Nav.com — Your Business Credit Monitoring Hub

**What it does:** Shows you your D&B, Experian Business, AND Equifax Business scores in one dashboard. Also shows financing options you qualify for based on your actual scores.

**Cost:** Free plan available (limited). Premium is ~$24.99/month.

**Recommendation:** Start with the free plan. Upgrade when you have active vendor accounts and need to monitor score changes.

# CHAPTER 3: NET-30 VENDOR ACCOUNTS — THE BUILDING BLOCKS

This is where the magic happens. Net-30 vendor accounts are trade accounts that:
1. Extend you credit (net-30 means you have 30 days to pay)
2. Report your payment history to business credit bureaus
3. Don't require a personal credit check (most of them)

You buy stuff you need for your business, pay within 30 days, and every on-time payment builds your Paydex score.

## The Net-30 Strategy

**Month 1:** Open 3-4 starter vendor accounts. Place small orders ($25-$75 each). Pay within 15 days (early payment = higher Paydex score).

**Month 2:** Place second orders with each vendor. Pay early again. Your accounts now have 2 months of payment history reporting.

**Month 3:** Open 2-3 more vendor accounts. You now have 5-7 accounts reporting. Your Paydex score starts forming.

**Month 4-6:** Continue ordering and paying early. Request credit limit increases on existing accounts. Your Paydex score should be 80+ by now.

> **The golden rule:** Pay EARLY. Not on time. Early. A payment received on day 15 of a net-30 account gives you a higher Paydex score than paying on day 28. Day 1 payment = 100 Paydex. Day 30 = 80 Paydex. After day 30 = below 80. Early is everything.

## SBA Loans (The Gold Standard)

SBA loans offer the best terms — lower interest rates, longer repayment, higher amounts. But they require preparation.

### SBA 7(a) Loan
- **Amount:** Up to $5 million
- **Interest:** Prime + 2.25-4.75%
- **Term:** 10-25 years
- **Requirements:** Good personal credit (680+), 2+ years in business, demonstrated ability to repay, business plan
- **Best for:** Major business investments, expansion, equipment

### SBA Microloan
- **Amount:** Up to $50,000
- **Interest:** 8-13% typically
- **Term:** Up to 6 years
- **Requirements:** Lower than 7(a) — newer businesses can qualify
- **Applied through:** Community Development Financial Institutions (CDFIs), not directly through SBA

### SBA 504 Loan
- **Amount:** Up to $5.5 million
- **For:** Real estate, large equipment
- **Down payment:** As low as 10%

## CDFIs (Community Development Financial Institutions)

These are MASSIVELY underutilized. CDFIs are nonprofit lenders that specifically serve businesses that traditional banks won't:

- Lower credit score requirements
- Smaller loan amounts available ($500-$50,000)
- Financial coaching included
- More flexible terms

Find CDFIs near you at **ofn.org/cdfi-locator**

# CHAPTER 7: MISTAKES THAT KILL BUSINESS CREDIT

## Mistake #1: Mixing Personal and Business Finances
Use your business bank account for ALL business expenses. Use your personal account for personal expenses. Never mix them. This is called "piercing the corporate veil" and it can destroy your LLC's liability protection.

## Mistake #2: Paying Net-30 Accounts on Day 29
On time is the bare minimum. Early is what builds an elite score. Pay on day 10-15 for maximum Paydex impact.

## Mistake #3: Not Verifying That Vendors Report
Not all vendors report to business bureaus. Before opening an account, confirm they report to at least one bureau (preferably D&B). Ask directly: "Do you report trade payment history to Dun & Bradstreet?"

## Mistake #4: Applying for Business Cards Too Early
Wait until you have at least 5 vendor accounts and a Paydex score of 80+ before applying for business credit cards. Applications with no business credit history get denied, and the hard inquiry hurts your personal score (since most biz cards check personal credit initially).

## Mistake #5: Ignoring Your D-U-N-S Number
Register with D&B EARLY. The D-U-N-S number is the foundation of your business credit identity. Many vendors won't report if you don't have one.

## Mistake #6: Not Having a Business Phone and Address
Lenders and vendors verify your business existence. A dedicated phone number (Google Voice is free) and a consistent address on all applications matters. Inconsistencies = denial.
